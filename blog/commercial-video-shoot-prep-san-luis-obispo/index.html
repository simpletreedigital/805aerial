How to Prepare for a Commercial Video Production Shoot in San Luis Obispo County

A practical checklist for business owners booking their first commercial video shoot on the Central Coast.

Published November 2026 · 805 Aerial · 8 min read

Most of the delay and extra cost on a commercial video shoot doesn't come from the camera work. It comes from a location that isn't ready, staff who weren't told what to expect, or a goal that was never clearly defined before the crew showed up. A few hours of preparation before a San Luis Obispo County commercial video production shoot usually pays for itself many times over on shoot day.

Know Your Goal Before You Book

A video meant to run as a paid social ad needs different pacing and length than one meant to sit on a homepage as a brand introduction. Before booking, decide where the finished video will actually live: a hero video on the website, a 15-second paid ad, a longer brand story for email and sales, or some mix of all three. That answer shapes everything from the shot list to how many setups are worth the time on location.

Get Your Location Shoot-Ready

A business's everyday working space usually isn't camera-ready without a pass of cleanup. Before the crew arrives:

  • Clear visible clutter. Storage boxes, cables, and personal items in the background read clearly on camera even when they're easy to ignore in person.
  • Check signage and branding. Faded, crooked, or missing signage shows up in wide and establishing shots.
  • Plan for crew parking and access. Downtown San Luis Obispo and tight commercial lots in Morro Bay can be the single biggest time cost of the day if access isn't sorted out in advance.
  • Think about lighting. Harsh midday sun through storefront windows or dim interior lighting both change what's possible; flag this when scheduling rather than discovering it on arrival.

Line Up the Right People

If an owner or staff member is speaking on camera, give them the questions or talking points in advance rather than asking them to improvise cold. Schedule the shoot for a time when the people who need to appear are actually available and not pulled into regular operations. If customers or the public may end up in frame, a business should think through whether that's acceptable for the shoot, since footage intended for public marketing use generally should avoid capturing identifiable members of the public without their knowledge.

A Simple Pre-Shoot Checklist

  • Decide where the video will run before the shoot, not after
  • Clean and declutter the main shooting area
  • Confirm who from the team will be on camera and when they're available
  • Sort out crew parking and site access in advance
  • Flag any lighting quirks (storefront glare, dim back rooms) ahead of time
Local note: coastal businesses near Morro Bay and Pismo Beach often get the clearest, most marketable light once the marine layer burns off midday, so a shoot scheduled too early can miss the best window.

What Happens After the Shoot

Once filming wraps, the project moves into editing, where ground footage, any aerial establishing shots, and interviews are cut together, color graded, and paired with licensed music. A full breakdown of that process and what a complete commercial video package includes is covered on our commercial video production page. Many projects also pull in a standalone aerial drone video pass or still drone photography for additional marketing assets from the same visit, which is worth deciding on before the crew books their time rather than adding it as an afterthought.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance should a business book a commercial video shoot?

Booking a few weeks ahead gives enough time to plan the shot list, confirm staff availability, and prepare the location properly rather than rushing into a shoot.

Do we need to close for business during filming?

Not usually. Most commercial shoots work around normal operating hours, though a business may want to schedule around its slowest period to reduce disruption and background noise.

What if we don't have anyone comfortable speaking on camera?

Plenty of effective commercial videos rely entirely on voiceover, text, and strong visuals rather than an on-camera spokesperson. This is worth discussing before the shoot so the plan accounts for it.

Can we get shorter social media cuts from the same shoot?

Yes, most commercial video projects are planned to produce a primary edit along with shorter vertical cuts for Instagram and other social platforms from the same footage.
